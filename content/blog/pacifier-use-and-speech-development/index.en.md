> Written in July 2024 for an academic writing course.

## Introduction

The purpose of this study is to examine the impact of pacifier use on speech development of infants. A long-term tracking method is expected to be employed to monitor daily pacifier use among young children aged 0-36 months. The relationship between the timing and frequency of pacifier use and the development of speech ability will be measured through regular speech assessments. 

## Background

A study conducted in the UK explored the impact of pacifier use on infant speech development. This study primarily relied on parent reports to gather information on infants’ and young children’s living conditions, the frequency of pacifier use, and other relevant family background details. The questionnaire results were combined with the Diagnostic Evaluation of Articulation and Phonology (DEAP) to assess whether pacifier use influenced young children’s speech abilities and to determine if the frequency of use was related to this impact. The results indicated that most aspects of speech abilities were not significantly related to pacifier use. However, a significant correlation was found between an increase in atypical speech errors and a higher frequency of pacifier use during the day. This phenomenon was not observed in children older than 38 months, which may be attributed to the small sample size of children over 38 months or to the fact that age-related language development at this stage may outweigh the impact of pacifier use. 

## Limitations

Although this study did not find a clear correlation between pacifier use and language development, only a correlation between atypical errors, this may be due to some limitations of the study itself, several of which are listed below. 

1. Accuracy of parental recall This study was a cross-sectional study on children of different ages at the same time, so recall bias is likely to occur. First, if the child is older, parents may not have a clear memory of the frequency of pacifier use during birth. However, pacifier use at this time may have a greater impact on language development. Furthermore, parents may not know the actual use situation because of their own opinions on whether a pacifier should be used. 

2. Measurement only at single word level When this study measured young children's speech ability, it only tested in single words level and did not examine the ability of connected speech level. However, compared with the use of single words, when young children use multiple consecutive words or sentences, they may be more likely to make errors. This may be one of the reasons why the research results show that the relationship between pacifier use and speech development is not significant. 

3. Insufficient detail in surveys of pacifier use In the questionnaire used in this study, questions about the frequency of pacifier use only inquired about the duration of daily use and the primary periods of use (morning, afternoon, evening, before bed, during sleep, and other). However, the questionnaire did not address the specific timing of use or the current environment in which the pacifier was used. These environmental variables could be relevant, as they may influence whether pacifier use occurs in contexts where speech development is actively practiced, potentially affecting young children’s speech abilities. Based on the above considerations, to minimize recall bias, this study aims to employ a regular follow-up longitudinal approach. This method will involve collecting more detailed data for each variable, including continuous speech assessments and a questionnaire that captures detailed information about pacifier use situations. 

## Method

## Participants and procedure

This study is expected to recruit 50 babies and parents. Starting from the birth of the baby, the baby's speech ability will be tested every month, and parents will also be asked to fill in a questionnaire on pacifier use. In the monthly tracking, children’s speech abilities will be assessed using the Diagnostic Evaluation of Articulation and Phonology (DEAP), focusing on both single-word and connected speech tasks. Consistent with the previous research, all aspects of children’s speech will be evaluated according to DEAP guidelines. Errors will be categorized into three types for subsequent analysis: age-

appropriate, delayed, or atypical. As for the parent questionnaire will gather basic personal and family background information, as well as detailed data on pacifier use. It will ask about when the pacifier is used throughout the day, including morning, afternoon, before bed, and during sleep. Additionally, it will inquire about daily usage habits, such as where the pacifier is given and whether it is used when the baby is crying. 

## Analysis

This study aims to employ multiple regression analysis as the primary analytical method. The analysis will use the frequency, timing, and context of pacifier use as independent variables, while the speech test results, categorized by different types of errors, will serve as the dependent variables. Additionally, the analysis will be conducted separately for different ages to examine whether the impact of pacifier use on speech development varies with age. 

## Conclusion

Ultimately, this study aims to determine the extent to which pacifier use affects speech development in young children, with a specific focus on coherent speech. If an effect is observed, the study will also investigate the age at which it occurs, whether the effect diminishes with age, and which factors may influence this phenomenon. However, this research method has some limitations. Primarily, these include constraints related to sample size and cultural and socioeconomic factors. The study did not account for cross-class and cross-cultural variables due to its scale and complexity, and the original study included only 50 samples. Although data may accumulate over time, the total sample size remains relatively small compared to the overall population of infants and young children. If this study finds an impact of pacifier use on speech development, a larger-scale investigation may be conducted to explore these effects.
