> Reflective writing for ATS1254 Culture, Power and Difference at Monash University, 2026.

"Language sits in Country... Language is Country, I am not sure Country can hear English." What moved me in this statement from the week4 lecture was that I realize languages are something more than tools of communication, they carry a framework of understanding the world. For instance, the Yanyuwa classification of dugong, distinguishing lone males, dominant bulls, and dugongs at different reproductive stages, and the pronoun that precisely distinguishes "you two, but not me" from "all of you, but not me," are not ornaments of a language. Instead, they represent a deep knowledge of land and relationships. For me, country does not fail to hear English because English is insufficient. It does not hear English simply because English does not contain the places for these perspectives. This difficulty runs in both directions. For speakers of Indigenous languages, English equally carries many unfamiliar meanings. For instance, “country” is not an abstract national concept. It is the spirit place where one truly belongs. Even the same word, across different language backgrounds, can carry different semantic domains, and those domains are often unreachable, leaving those who do not carry them to struggle at the edges.

What really interests me here is that this gap between domains does not exist only between different languages. It also exists within a single language. As a non-native speaker, I used to believe that expanding my vocabulary was crucial to learn English, as it gave me more options to express my thoughts. However, I later realized the challenge is much deeper. I struggled with entering many sets of habits and perspectives which I had never been raised inside. Just like academic English, everyday English, and the English used in different fields can each carry their own structures and word choices. Even as the lecture reminds us that “English is a language, not a sign of intelligence or a sign of someone's character or integrity,” people still often use it to judge intelligence between different languages, sometimes, within the same language.

Ironically, what I am actually doing when I write this piece is learning both how to express my thoughts and how to express them in a form that is recognised as clear and convincing. This form belongs to people of a particular social position. Those who had the opportunity to grow up inside these habits of language do not need to consciously learn that they exist, while, at the same time, those who must deliberately learn them are often evaluated and defined by them. Perhaps, in the end, I am not sure if I can fully hear English either.
